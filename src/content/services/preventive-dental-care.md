## What it is

Preventive care is everything done to stop dental problems starting, or to
catch them while they are still small: routine examinations, X-rays where
indicated, fluoride application, fissure sealants for children, and advice
tailored to your own risk.

## Why it matters more than anything else we do

Tooth decay and gum disease both develop quietly. Neither hurts in its early
stages, which is precisely the stage at which both are simplest and cheapest to
treat. By the time a tooth aches, the options have usually narrowed and the
cost has usually risen.

A filling placed early is a small procedure. The same tooth left for two years
may need a root canal and a crown. The dentistry has not become better in the
meantime — the problem has just grown.

## What a preventive visit involves

- Examination of every tooth, the gums, and the soft tissues of the mouth
- X-rays if there is a reason to look between or beneath the teeth
- An assessment of your individual risk — diet, brushing, smoking, dry mouth,
  medical conditions such as diabetes
- Cleaning where needed (see [scaling and polishing](/services/scaling-and-polishing/))
- Practical advice you can actually apply

## For children

Children benefit from fissure sealants — a thin protective coating over the
grooves of the back teeth, where decay most often starts. Fluoride application
strengthens developing enamel. Both are quick, painless and considerably
cheaper than treating the decay they prevent.

Bringing a child early, before anything hurts, also means their first
experience of a dental clinic is an uneventful one. That matters more than it
sounds.

## How often

Most people are seen every six months. Your dentist may suggest a different
interval depending on your gum health, decay history, smoking, and any medical
conditions that affect the mouth.
