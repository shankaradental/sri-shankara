## What it is

A bridge replaces a missing tooth by anchoring a replacement to the teeth on
either side of the gap. Those neighbouring teeth are prepared and crowned, with
the replacement tooth joined between them. The whole unit is cemented in place
and is not removed for cleaning.

## Why replace a missing tooth at all

A gap is not only cosmetic. Teeth either side of it gradually drift into the
space and the opposing tooth can over-erupt, which changes the bite over years
and makes later treatment more complicated. Chewing also shifts to the other
side, loading it more heavily.

## What the appointments involve

**First visit.** The supporting teeth are prepared under local anaesthetic, an
impression or digital scan is taken, and a temporary bridge is fitted.

**Second visit.** The permanent bridge is checked for fit, shade and bite, then
cemented.

## Afterwards

The one thing that decides how long a bridge lasts is cleaning underneath the
replacement tooth. Floss cannot pass between the units, so a floss threader or
an interdental brush is needed to clean under the span. Plaque collecting there
is the usual reason a bridge fails years later — not the bridge itself
breaking.

## Bridge or implant?

An **implant** replaces the missing tooth on its own, without touching the
neighbouring teeth. That is a real advantage when those teeth are sound and
undamaged — a bridge means preparing two healthy teeth to fix one gap. Implants
take longer and cost more, and need adequate bone.

A **bridge** is quicker, needs no surgery, and makes sense when the
neighbouring teeth already need crowns anyway.

A **removable partial denture** is the least invasive and least expensive
option.

The dentist will go through which applies to your case after an examination.
