## What it is

Orthodontic treatment moves teeth gradually into better alignment. It addresses
crowding, spacing, and how the upper and lower teeth meet.

## Options

**Fixed braces** — brackets bonded to the teeth connected by a wire. Suitable for
the widest range of cases, including complex bite corrections.

**Clear aligners** — a series of removable trays. Less visible and removable for
eating, but they depend entirely on being worn the prescribed number of hours a
day, and not every case is suitable for them.

## When to start

Children are usually assessed around age 7–9, when it becomes possible to see
how the permanent teeth and jaws are developing. Treatment itself often begins
later. Adults can be treated at any age provided the gums and supporting bone
are healthy.

## What is involved

An assessment with X-rays, photographs and impressions or a scan, then a plan
with expected duration. Adjustment visits follow every few weeks.

## Afterwards

Retainers are needed once active treatment finishes. Teeth drift back towards
their original position without them — this is normal biology, not a failure of
treatment, and retainer wear is a long-term commitment.
