## What it is

A routine visit with two parts: an examination of your teeth, gums and soft
tissues, and a professional clean that removes the hardened plaque (tartar) that
brushing cannot shift.

## Why it matters

Gum disease is common and often painless in its early stages, which is why it is
usually found at a check-up rather than noticed at home. Early decay is also
easier and cheaper to treat before it reaches the nerve.

## What the appointment involves

The dentist examines each tooth, checks the gums, and looks at the soft tissues
of the mouth. X-rays may be taken if there is a reason to look between or below
the teeth. Scaling uses an ultrasonic instrument to lift tartar from the tooth
surface and just below the gumline, followed by polishing.

## Afterwards

Gums may feel slightly tender and teeth briefly more sensitive for a day or two.
This settles. If your gums bleed during brushing afterwards, keep brushing
gently — bleeding usually reduces as inflammation improves.

## How often

Most people are seen every six months. Your dentist may suggest a different
interval based on your gum health, decay history, smoking, and medical
conditions such as diabetes.
