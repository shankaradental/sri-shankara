## What they are

Dentures are removable appliances that replace missing teeth and the tissue
around them.

**Complete dentures** replace all the teeth in one or both jaws.

**Partial dentures** fill several gaps and clip onto the remaining natural
teeth.

## Why they are still a good option

Dentures are the least invasive and least expensive way to replace multiple
missing teeth. No surgery, no preparation of neighbouring teeth. Where several
teeth are missing across an arch, they often make more sense than bridging
every gap.

## What is involved

Fitting a denture takes several visits: impressions, a bite registration, a
try-in in wax so shape and appearance can be adjusted before it is finished,
then the fit itself. The staged approach exists so that changes are made before
the denture is made permanent, not after.

## The adjustment period — and this is worth knowing in advance

A new denture feels bulky and strange at first. Speech takes a week or two to
normalise; reading aloud at home speeds it up considerably. Eating starts with
soft food cut small, chewing on both sides, and builds from there. Some sore
spots are normal in the first weeks.

Come back for adjustments rather than enduring soreness. Small relief of a
pressure point takes minutes and makes the difference between a denture that
gets worn and one that lives in a drawer.

## Care

Clean dentures over a basin of water or a folded towel — they break easily on a
hard sink. Use a denture brush and soap or a denture cleaner, not ordinary
toothpaste, which is too abrasive. Leave them out overnight in water so the
tissues underneath get a rest.

Keep seeing the dentist even with complete dentures. The gums and bone beneath
change shape over years, which is why a denture that fitted well eventually
loosens and needs relining or remaking. Oral tissues also need checking.

## Alternatives

Implant-retained dentures use two or more implants to hold a denture firmly in
place, which solves the stability problem for lower dentures in particular.
More expensive, and dependent on adequate bone.
