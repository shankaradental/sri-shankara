## What they are

A veneer is a thin shell bonded to the front surface of a tooth. Veneers are
used to change the colour, shape, size or apparent alignment of the teeth that
show when you smile.

## What they can address

- Teeth that stay discoloured after whitening, or discoloured from within
- Chipped or worn front edges
- Small gaps between front teeth
- Teeth that are slightly out of line, where full orthodontics is not wanted
- Teeth that are misshapen or narrower than their neighbours

## Porcelain or composite

**Porcelain veneers** are made in a laboratory and bonded at a second visit.
They resist staining well and hold their appearance longer.

**Composite veneers** are built up directly on the tooth in a single visit.
Less expensive and more easily repaired, but more prone to staining over time.

## What is involved

Most porcelain veneers require a small amount of enamel to be removed from the
front of the tooth so the veneer sits flush rather than looking bulky. **That
part is irreversible** — once enamel is removed the tooth will always need
covering. This is the single most important thing to understand before
committing, and the dentist will discuss it with you properly.

A trial or mock-up is usually done first so you can see the proposed shape
before anything is prepared.

## Afterwards

Veneers are cleaned like natural teeth. Avoid biting nails, pens or packaging
with them — porcelain is strong in compression but can chip under a sharp point
load. If you grind your teeth at night, a guard is usually recommended to
protect them.

## Honest expectations

Veneers change appearance; they do not make a tooth healthier. Decay or gum
disease must be treated first. Results depend on your starting point, and the
final shade of porcelain cannot be changed once made — which is why the shade
is agreed carefully beforehand.
