## What it is

Digital radiography captures dental X-ray images on a sensor rather than film.
The image appears on screen within seconds and can be enlarged and adjusted.

## Why X-rays are taken at all

A visual examination shows the surfaces the dentist can see. A great deal
happens where they cannot:

- Decay starting between two teeth, where they touch
- Decay underneath an existing filling
- Bone loss from gum disease
- Infection at the tip of a root
- Unerupted or impacted teeth, including wisdom teeth
- The position of nerves and sinuses before extraction or implant placement

## The advantages over film

**Lower radiation dose.** Digital sensors need considerably less exposure than
film to produce a usable image.

**Immediate.** No processing, so the image is on screen while you are still in
the chair — which means you can see what the dentist is describing rather than
taking it on trust.

**Adjustable.** Contrast and magnification can be changed to examine a specific
area without another exposure.

**Comparable over time.** Stored images sit alongside earlier ones, so slow
changes are easier to spot.

## Is it safe?

The dose from a dental X-ray is small. Radiographs are taken only when there is
a clinical reason — not routinely at every visit — and a lead apron and thyroid
collar are used where appropriate.

**Tell the dentist if you are pregnant or may be pregnant.** Non-urgent
radiographs are normally deferred.
