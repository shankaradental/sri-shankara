## What it is

A crown is a custom-made covering that fits over a tooth like a cap, restoring
its shape and protecting what remains of the natural tooth underneath.

## When it is recommended

- The tooth is too broken down for a filling to hold reliably
- After root canal treatment on a back tooth — treated teeth become more
  brittle and are prone to splitting under chewing load
- A cracked tooth that needs holding together
- A badly worn or discoloured tooth where a filling would not restore the shape
- Over a dental implant

## Materials

**Porcelain** — best colour match, used where appearance matters most.

**Zirconia** — very strong and still tooth-coloured; a good choice for back
teeth that take heavy load.

**Porcelain bonded to metal** — a metal core for strength with a porcelain
outer layer.

The dentist will recommend based on where the tooth sits, how much force it
takes, and how visible it is.

## What the appointments involve

**First visit.** The tooth is prepared under local anaesthetic — reshaped so
the crown fits over it — then an impression or digital scan is taken and a
temporary crown fitted.

**Second visit.** The temporary comes off, the permanent crown is checked for
fit, shade and bite, then cemented in place.

## Afterwards

Temporary crowns are lightly cemented on purpose. Avoid sticky and hard foods
on that side, and slide floss out sideways rather than pulling upwards. If a
temporary comes off, keep it and call the clinic — it needs re-fitting so the
prepared tooth does not drift.

Once the permanent crown is on, clean it exactly as you would a natural tooth.
The crown cannot decay, but the natural tooth at its margin can, so the gumline
is where brushing and flossing matter most.

## How long they last

Well-made crowns commonly last many years. Lifespan depends on the bite, on
grinding habits, and on how clean the margin is kept. They are checked at every
routine visit.
