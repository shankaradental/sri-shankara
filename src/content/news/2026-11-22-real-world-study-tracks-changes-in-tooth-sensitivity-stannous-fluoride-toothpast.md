## Understanding Tooth Sensitivity and Daily Impact

Dentine hypersensitivity, commonly referred to as tooth sensitivity, occurs when underlying dentine—the inner tissue of the tooth beneath the outer protective enamel—becomes exposed. This exposure can lead to sharp discomfort or pain when teeth encounter cold, hot, sweet, or acidic stimuli. Beyond occasional discomfort, persistent tooth sensitivity can influence daily activities, such as eating, drinking, and oral care routines, thereby affecting a person's oral health-related quality of life.

To observe how self-reported sensitivity and related quality-of-life metrics change over time in everyday settings, researchers conducted a 24-week real-world study involving adults using a commercially available toothpaste containing stannous fluoride.

## How the 24-Week Real-World Study Was Designed

The study was designed as a fully decentralized, prospective, open-label real-world trial. This meant that participants were not required to attend in-clinic dental visits; instead, all assessments were completed remotely through an online mobile application.

A total of 536 healthy adults aged 18 to 65 who self-reported experiencing tooth sensitivity were recruited via digital platforms. Of the enrolled group, 498 participants reported initiating use of the study product, and 460 completed the entire 24-week period.

Participants were provided with Sensodyne Repair & Protect toothpaste (formulated with 0.454% anhydrous stannous fluoride, yielding 1,100 parts per million of fluoride) in its standard commercial packaging. They were instructed to incorporate the toothpaste into their normal daily oral hygiene routines following the instructions on the package.

To track changes over time, participants filled out the validated Dentine Hypersensitivity Experience Questionnaire (DHEQ-48) every four weeks for 24 weeks. The DHEQ-48 measures the impact of tooth sensitivity on daily life across five specific domains, with a maximum possible total score of 238 (where higher scores indicate a greater negative impact from sensitivity). Additionally, participants recorded their pain levels using a 10-point Numeric Pain Rating Scale (NPRS) and rated their satisfaction at the end of the study.

## Key Findings from Participant Reports

The collected data showed progressive reductions in self-reported sensitivity scores and improvements in quality-of-life measures over the course of six months:

- **DHEQ Total Scores:** At baseline (the start of the study), the mean DHEQ total score was 176.3 (with a standard error of 1.42). By week 4, the mean score had decreased to 154.3 (standard error 1.84). By week 24, it further reduced to 113.9 (standard error 2.37). Both decreases were statistically significant compared to baseline, with consistent improvements reported across all five questionnaire domains.
- **Pain Rating Scores:** Pain reported on the Numeric Pain Rating Scale dropped from a mean of 5.7 (standard error 0.10) at baseline to 4.1 (standard error 0.10) at week 4, showing continued reduction through week 24.
- **Overall Experience:** Participants recorded that their tooth sensitivity became progressively less intense, less bothersome, and more tolerable as the 24-week study advanced.

## Study Features and Limitations

When reviewing these findings, several aspects of the study design should be considered:

- **Absence of a Control Group:** The study was open-label and single-arm, meaning all participants used the active product and knew which product they were testing. It did not include a control group using a non-fluoride or non-desensitizing placebo toothpaste for comparison.
- **Self-Reported Measures:** All data relied on self-reported questionnaires and pain scales completed on an app. No in-person clinical examinations or objective tactile/cold-air sensitivity tests were performed by dentists to verify clinical baseline levels or changes.
- **Digital Recruitment:** The study recruited adults through digital platforms based on self-reported sensitivity rather than a clinical diagnosis in a dental practice setting.

## What This Means for Patients

This study provides real-world observational data showing that adults using a 0.454% anhydrous stannous fluoride toothpaste as part of their routine home care reported steady reductions in tooth sensitivity and less disruption to their daily lives over 24 weeks.

Because tooth pain and sensitivity can stem from a variety of causes—including enamel erosion, exposed root surfaces, cracks, or dental decay—individuals experiencing persistent tooth discomfort should consult a dentist. A professional evaluation can help determine the underlying source of sensitivity and establish an appropriate oral care strategy.
