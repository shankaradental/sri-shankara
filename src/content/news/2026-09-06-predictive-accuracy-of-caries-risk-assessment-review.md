## Predicting Tooth Decay Before It Starts

Preventing tooth decay—clinically referred to as dental caries—is a central goal of modern dentistry. To stop cavities before they develop, dentists often try to evaluate a patient's likelihood of developing new lesions over time. This process is known as caries risk assessment (CRA).

Rather than waiting for cavities to form, risk assessment models combine multiple factors—such as dietary habits, oral hygiene, past dental history, and exposure to fluoride—to classify an individual as low, moderate, or high risk. A systematic review examined a decade of published research to determine how accurately these prediction tools perform in real-world clinical settings.

## How the Review Was Conducted

The researchers followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA 2020) guidelines to update an earlier review published in 2015. They searched five major scientific databases—PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, Embase, and Scopus—spanning studies published between February 2015 and July 2025.

To be included, studies had to monitor participants for at least one year and report enough statistical data to calculate accuracy measures. Out of 7,332 initial records identified, 30 studies met the inclusion criteria, and 16 provided sufficient comparable data to be combined into a meta-analysis. The researchers examined single-factor predictors, multi-factor scoring systems, and newer machine learning models across both children and adults.

## What the Research Found About Different Assessment Tools

The review evaluated several widely used risk assessment systems, looking closely at their sensitivity (how well a tool identifies people who will actually develop cavities) and specificity (how well it correctly identifies people who will stay cavity-free).

* **The Cariogram (in children):** This multi-factor tool demonstrated a pooled sensitivity of 72.10% and a specificity of 69.96%, showing a balanced ability to identify both high-risk and low-risk young patients.
* **Caries Management by Risk Assessment (CAMBRA):** This protocol achieved a sensitivity of 67.65% and a specificity of 58.96%, though the studies evaluating it showed substantial variation in their results.
* **American Academy of Paediatric Dentistry (AAPD) Tool:** The AAPD risk assessment tool showed a very high sensitivity of 95.56%, meaning it rarely missed a child who went on to develop decay. However, its specificity was very low at 5.12%, indicating that it frequently classified children as high-risk even when they did not develop new cavities. The authors noted this makes the AAPD tool useful for broad initial screening rather than precise risk categorization.
* **Past Caries Experience:** Looking simply at whether a patient had cavities in the past demonstrated moderate predictive accuracy on its own.
* **Machine Learning:** Artificial intelligence and computer algorithms showed promising potential in predicting decay, but there were not enough uniform studies available to combine their results in the statistical analysis.

## Study Limitations and Considerations

Overall, the systematic review concluded that current caries risk assessment methods show moderate predictive accuracy. The findings highlight significant variability depending on the specific tool used, the study design, and the population evaluated. Because many tools either over-predict or under-predict risk, structured assessments cannot replace the clinical evaluation of an individual dentist.

The authors highlighted that future improvements may come from longer prospective studies and the potential addition of objective biological markers to risk calculation formulas.

## What This Means for Patients

Risk assessment questionnaires and screening forms can give dentists a structured way to understand your personal risk factors for cavities. However, no calculation tool provides an absolute prediction of whether you will or will not develop tooth decay in the coming years. Regular clinical check-ups, personal oral hygiene habits, and individualized guidance from your dentist remain the most dependable approach to maintaining oral health.
