When planning orthodontic treatments that require temporary skeletal anchorage devices, such as miniscrews, understanding the thickness and quality of the underlying jawbone is essential. A recently published retrospective cross-sectional comparative study investigated the structural characteristics of the posterior upper jaw in patients with unilateral cleft lip and palate compared to individuals without clefts.

### Study Design and Imaging Evaluation

The research evaluated 66 nonsyndromic patients with unilateral cleft lip and palate (comprising 29 males and 37 females with a mean age of approximately 19 years) alongside 66 age- and sex-matched noncleft controls. Researchers utilized cone-beam computed tomography, a specialized three-dimensional imaging technique, to measure morphometric parameters across the dental arches.

Cortical bone thickness—the dense outer layer of bone that provides stability for orthodontic anchorage—was measured on both the cheek (buccal) and roof-of-the-mouth (palatal) aspects. Measurements were taken at three specific vertical levels (4, 6, and 8 mm apical to the alveolar crest, which is the ridge of bone containing the tooth sockets) across the premolar and molar regions.

### What the Study Found

The evaluation revealed several distinct patterns in bone architecture:

* **Cleft-Side Deficiencies:** Cortical bone thickness was significantly reduced on the cleft side compared to the noncleft side and the control group across nearly all measured sites. Statistical analysis indicated very large effect sizes, with mean bone deficiencies ranging from 0.26 to 0.40 millimeters.
* **Palatal Thickness:** Interestingly, the palatal cortical thickness on the noncleft side was significantly greater in the cleft lip and palate group than in the control group.
* **Vertical and Regional Trends:** Across all participants, cortical thickness increased progressively the further away it was measured from the alveolar crest (moving from 4 mm up to 8 mm apically) and generally increased from the premolar regions toward the back molar regions.

The authors noted that the interradicular spaces between the back molars and the palatal sites offered the greatest potential cortical engagement for skeletal anchorage in these patients.

### Limitations of the Research

Because this was a retrospective cross-sectional study using imaging data from institutional and specialized care centers, the findings reflect anatomical measurements at a single point in time. While the sample size of 66 patients per group provided robust comparative data, clinical application requires individualized assessment for every patient.

### What This Means for Patients

Orthodontic treatment plans must account for individual anatomical variations, particularly for patients born with cleft lip and palate who may present with localized differences in jawbone thickness. Advanced three-dimensional imaging allows clinicians to carefully map bone dimensions before placing temporary anchorage devices, helping to identify the most suitable and stable sites within the upper jaw.
