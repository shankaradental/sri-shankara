More people are turning to conversational artificial intelligence (AI) chatbots when searching for medical and dental guidance online. In orthodontics—the field of dentistry focused on correcting misaligned teeth and jaws—patients frequently ask online tools about braces, clear aligners, and treatment timelines. However, whether these automated tools provide dependable, safe, and clinically sound information has remained uncertain. A systematic review gathered and analyzed data from recent investigations to assess the accuracy, readability, completeness, and safety of orthodontic advice generated by AI.

## How the Review Evaluated AI Chatbots

To examine how chatbots handle orthodontic queries, the review authors followed established research protocols, specifically the PRISMA 2020 guidelines and Cochrane systematic review standards. The researchers conducted broad searches across multiple electronic databases, including PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov, the Open Science Framework, and Google Scholar.

They sought studies that directly compared AI-generated answers against orthodontic specialists, recognized reference answers, or standardized evaluation tools. In total, 16 analytical cross-sectional studies met the selection criteria. To evaluate the quality of the included research, the authors used the Joanna Briggs Institute checklist to determine the risk of bias, and applied the GRADE approach (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation) to assess the overall certainty of the evidence.

## Where Chatbots Performed Well

Across the 16 included studies, AI chatbots generally performed well when answering routine orthodontic questions. The models generated coherent, easily understandable responses to patient frequently asked questions and foundational orthodontic concepts.

The review also noted that the readability of the AI-generated text was generally favorable. This means the language was structured in a way that was accessible and simple for lay readers to follow, without relying heavily on confusing academic jargon.

## Significant Clinical Limitations

Despite clear language, readability did not equate to clinical soundness. The review revealed that a well-written, easy-to-read answer could still be incomplete or clinically flawed. Chatbot performance varied widely depending on the specific model version used, how prompts were phrased, and the underlying complexity of the clinical scenario.

The authors identified major weaknesses in several critical areas:

- **Treatment planning:** Chatbots struggled to provide sound, step-by-step guidance on how orthodontic conditions should be managed.
- **Individualized recommendations:** AI tools could not reliably tailor advice to individual patient circumstances.
- **Contextual risk framing:** The programs often failed to explain the risks, trade-offs, and precautions associated with orthodontic care.
- **Completeness:** Answers frequently omitted essential background information needed to make an informed healthcare decision.

Most concerning were instances of inaccurate guidance. Several included studies documented incomplete or entirely false claims regarding clear aligners, the effects of treatment on the airway, long-term bite stability after treatment, and surgical orthodontic procedures.

## Study Limitations

The review noted several inherent limitations in the current scientific literature. Because the 16 studies used different chatbot systems, varying prompt designs, differing comparison standards, and inconsistent scoring tools, the data was too varied (heterogeneous) to combine into a statistical meta-analysis. Additionally, all 16 studies were cross-sectional in design, meaning they evaluated chatbot responses at a single snapshot in time rather than tracking real-world patient outcomes. Across the studies, the risk of bias ranged from low to moderate, and certainty of the evidence required careful interpretation.

## What This Means for Patients

Conversational AI can offer helpful, easy-to-read explanations of basic dental concepts and everyday questions. However, the research shows that AI cannot safely provide personalized advice, evaluate complex bite problems, or design a treatment plan.

Because chatbots can generate incomplete or inaccurate statements regarding clear aligners, stability, and surgery, patients should treat AI-generated answers as basic background reading rather than clinical guidance. Decisions about tooth movement, appliances, and orthodontic risks always require direct evaluation, examination, and diagnosis by a qualified dental professional.
