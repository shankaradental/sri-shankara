For many older adults receiving treatments for bone health, concerns about rare jaw complications can make dental visits a source of significant worry. A recent study published in 2026 explores a digital tool created to help patients address this anticipatory dental anxiety before stepping into a clinic.

### Understanding the Context

Older adults undergoing antiresorptive therapy—a common class of medications used to strengthen bones and manage conditions like osteoporosis—sometimes face concerns regarding medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw, known medically as MRONJ. This condition involves rare healing issues in the jawbone. While the actual risk can be managed with proper dental care, the worry alone can cause substantial distress and lead patients to avoid necessary dental check-ups.

To help bridge this communication gap, researchers developed a bilingual, web-based risk communication tool called *Dental Confidence*. The tool is designed specifically for older adults and presents structured information regarding dental care while taking bone-strengthening medications.

### What the Study Investigated

In this prospective single-arm study, researchers evaluated the feasibility, patient acceptance, and immediate outcomes of using the web-based tool. A total of 51 participants completed assessments before and immediately after using the application. Additionally, five participants took part in detailed interviews to provide context on their responses.

All 51 participants successfully completed the intervention and the required assessments. To measure anxiety levels, researchers used a Visual Analog Scale, commonly referred to as a VAS, where participants rate their feelings on a continuous scale.

### What the Researchers Found

Before using the digital tool, participants recorded a mean VAS anxiety score of 53.24. Immediately after using the application, the mean score dropped to 44.80. This reflected a statistically significant average reduction of 8.43 points. 

However, self-reported knowledge scores and communication confidence scores remained largely unchanged, averaging around 25.49 before use and 25.43 afterward. When asked about satisfaction, participants reported a high average score of 4.45 out of 5, with over 90 percent rating their satisfaction as a 4 or 5.

In follow-up interviews, participants noted that receiving clear reassurance, putting risks into proper perspective, experiencing visual comfort while reading the platform, and receiving communication support helped explain their positive responses to the tool.

### Important Limitations

While the findings show promise for reducing immediate anxiety scores in a supported setting, the study has notable limitations. It was a single-arm study involving 51 participants without a control group for direct comparison. Furthermore, the evaluation only measured immediate responses right after application use, meaning the long-term impact on patient behavior and ongoing anxiety remains unknown.

### What This Means for Patients

Managing anxiety about dental treatments is an important part of overall healthcare, particularly for individuals navigating complex medication regimens. While digital communication tools may help provide immediate reassurance and contextualize information about bone health therapies, more controlled studies with longer follow-up periods are needed to fully understand their long-term effectiveness in everyday clinical settings.
