Routine dental appointments offer an opportunity to monitor general health parameters alongside oral care. A recently published cross-sectional analysis investigated the use of point-of-care hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) screening in dental settings to identify elevated blood sugar levels among patients.

## Study Design and Screening Results

The study evaluated 904 adults who completed a pre-visit survey assessing their primary care engagement and any prior glycemic concerns. These participants then underwent point-of-care HbA1c screening, a quick test that measures average blood glucose levels over the preceding two to three months.

The findings showed that 27.3% of the adults evaluated had an elevated HbA1c level of 5.7% or higher. Within this group, 7.0% fell into the diabetes range, meaning their HbA1c was 6.5% or higher.

## Gaps in Awareness and Primary Care

One of the notable findings involved patient awareness. Among the 247 individuals who were found to have an elevated HbA1c reading, 60.7% reported having no prior awareness or concern regarding their blood sugar levels. For those whose readings fell specifically within the prediabetes range, that lack of prior knowledge rose to 70.1%.

Additionally, the study recorded data on routine medical care: 31.5% of the total participants reported that they were not engaged with primary care. Statistical analysis revealed that individuals who lacked primary care engagement had a 4.25-fold higher adjusted odds of reporting no prior glycemic concern compared to those who regularly saw a primary care provider.

## Limitations

As a cross-sectional study relying on a single screening event and patient surveys, the research captures data at one specific point in time and reflects a sample of adults seeking dental care. While it demonstrates associations between dental screening outcomes and primary care access, it does not evaluate long-term health outcomes following the identification of elevated HbA1c.

## What This Means for Patients

This research highlights how dental visits can occasionally intersect with broader health monitoring. Because a significant number of individuals with elevated blood sugar levels may be unaware of their status—particularly those who do not regularly visit a primary care physician—point-of-care screening in dental clinics may help bridge gaps in health awareness and encourage timely medical follow-up.
